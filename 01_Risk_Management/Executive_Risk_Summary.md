## EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW

I’ve reviewed the security and operational risks facing Fresh Brew Café. I identified 21 risks, ranging from low to critical. The three most important risks are related to **customer allergens, regulatory compliance, and equipment failure**. These can be addressed with practical and affordable controls. None of these risks requires major business changes; they require focused attention and some investment over the next few weeks.

## TOP 3 RISKS

### 1. CUSTOMER ALLERGENS

**What could happen:** Staff mistakenly contaminates a customer’s drink/food with gluten, peanut oil, dairy, etc. Customer unknowingly consumes and gets a severe allergic reaction, resulting in hospitalisation. The café is found culpable of negligence, and there’s social media backlash.

**How likely:** Medium. Allergies are common. Staff are poorly trained in food-handling procedures. Low staff turnover in peak service hours/days. Separation of duties – a single staff member doing almost everything becomes fatigued, disorganised, and disoriented.

**Impact if it happens:** If a customer reacts negatively to an allergy:

- Customer lawsuits and hospitalisation costs
- Regulatory fine: $5000-$10000 minimum
- Legal and compliance notification
- Lost customer trust and churn: significant revenue impact
- Reputational damage: customers avoid the café
- Potential revocation of business certificate and closure

**What I recommend:**

1. Install an allergen-tracking system (either a built-in POS feature ($50/month) or a low-tech alternative costing <$20 once-off).
2. Train staff in food handling procedures, especially allergens.
3. Implement physical controls (separate equipment and their cleaning stations).

**Cost to fix:**

- POS programming ($0): already integrated in the POS system’s modifier features to add “ALLERGY” tags to kitchen tickets.
- Color-coded equipment ($40-$60): buy 2 or 3 distinct brightly colored milk frothing pitchers to ensure they’re never mixed with dairy pitchers.
- Staff training ($0-$100): add a 15/30-minute allergy protocol review/training to the next pre-existing staff meeting.
- Warning stickers ($10): buy a roll of bright neon red “ALLERGY” stickers to place on outgoing cups/plates so the customer knows their request was handled carefully.

**Residual risk after fixes:** Medium-to-Low. With these controls in place, even if a supplier changes the ingredients in a syrup to suddenly include a nut extract, the manager updates the system once, and it automatically flags every menu item that uses the syrup (Recipe and Ingredient traceability).

### 2. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE

**What could happen:** A severe pest infestation (rodents, insects) creates an unsafe and unhealthy working environment, leading to the contamination of food products, resulting in staff and customer illness, potential health department closure, and severe reputational damage.

**How likely:** High. You’re not complying with regulatory requirements partly because you’re located in a semi-urban area where regulatory inspectors seldom come for inspection. Again, there is no stiff competition; hence, the community have no choice but to patronize your café.

**Impact if it happens:**

- Staff and customer illness is the most direct impact: Pests carry pathogens (e.g., Salmonella, E. coli, etc.). Consumption of contaminated food can lead to severe food poisoning or disease outbreaks among your staff and community.
- Fines and lawsuits: The business will face heavy regulatory fines. Furthermore, sick customers (or employees) could sue the café for medical costs and negligence.
- Financial impact: You will face emergency costs. You will have to pay premium prices for emergency extermination, specialized deep-cleaning services, and the cost of throwing away and replacing all contaminated food inventory. There is also immediate revenue loss when the café is closed.
- Reputational impact: There is a possibility of brand damage. A customer (or some employees) seeing a mouse will likely record it and post it on social media. A health department closure sign on the front door is also highly visible to the public, leading to loss of trust.

**What I recommend:**

1. Document onboarding and training: Train new hires on proper food handling and document the process for future use/reference.
2. Assign and empower one of the staff as a compliance officer who will see to it that the café complies with the regulatory requirements.
3. Implement monthly internal audits by setting up a compliance calendar to track regulatory measures.

**Cost to fix:** $50-$150/month (recurring commercial pest control contract) or $250-$500/year (one-time upfront payment).

**Residual risk after fixes:** Medium-to-Low. Assigning a compliance officer who enforces regulatory measures, putting in place monthly audits and documenting onboarding and training will reduce the risk from high to medium or possibly low.

### 3. EQUIPMENT FAILURE

**What could happen:** The café’s primary espresso machine breaks down in the middle of the morning rush due to a blown gasket. Alternatively, the commercial refrigerator’s compressor dies overnight after the last employee leaves, meaning the temperature slowly rises over 12 hours.

**How likely:** High. Commercial café equipment is subjected to heavy, daily use. Without a strict preventative maintenance schedule, the likelihood of a breakdown due to simple wear-and-tear is nearly guaranteed over time.

**Impact if it happens:**

- Espresso machine: if this fails, the café immediately loses its primary revenue stream. Customers will walk out, resulting in a massive drop in daily sales and immediate customer churn.
- Refrigerator: if it fails overnight, the entire inventory of food products and perishable ingredients will spoil. This requires thousands of dollars to replace and halts food service entirely until the unit is fixed and restocked.

**What I recommend:**

1. Emergency vendor list: keep a list of 24/7 emergency repair technicians posted in the back office.
2. Temperature alarms: install inexpensive, WiFi-enabled temperature sensors in commercial fridges that send a text message to the manager if the temperature exceeds safe levels.
3. Preventative maintenance contract: sign an agreement with a local technician to service the espresso machine quarterly before parts fail.
4. Contingency plan: train staff on exactly what to do if the machine goes down.

**Cost to fix:**

- $30-100 one-time cost (temperature alarms)
- $300-$600/year (Preventative maintenance contract)

**Residual risk after fixes:** Medium-to-Low. You can never eliminate the risk of a machine breaking. However, by implementing these fixes, you drastically reduce the likelihood of it happening during maintenance, and you significantly reduce the impact because the temperature alarms will catch the fridge failing before the food spoils, and staff will know exactly how to handle the coffee downtime.

## OVERALL RISK POSTURE

Fresh Brew Café currently operates in a highly reactive state. While day-to-day operations function normally, the underlying security, compliance, and operational controls are informal or missing entirely. Management relies heavily on employee memory and basic trust rather than documented procedures. Because there is no preventative maintenance, no formal compliance tracking, and gaps in basic food safety handling, the café carries a high level of operational and financial risk. Many of these vulnerabilities represent single points of failure that could abruptly halt revenue or trigger severe legal liabilities.

## NEXT STEPS (In Priority Order)

1. **Address Health and Safety Gaps (Immediate):** Procure color-coded equipment for allergen separation and update the POS system to clearly flag allergy orders on kitchen tickets. Hold a mandatory staff meeting to establish strict food handling rules and "clean as you go" spill procedures.
2. **Establish Baseline Compliance (Within 14 Days):** Assign one manager to create a compliance calendar that tracks all health permits, fire inspections, and licensing renewals. Draft a basic, one-page employee policy outlining food safety standards and social media rules, requiring all staff to sign it.
3. **Secure Critical Equipment and Data (Within 30 Days):** Sign a preventative maintenance agreement for the espresso machine. Purchase and install WiFi temperature sensors for the commercial refrigerators. Set up automated, daily offsite backups for the café's main server.

## CONCLUSION

Fresh Brew Café is currently exposed to several critical risks that could lead to forced closure, lawsuits, or extended operational downtime. However, mitigating these issues does not require a massive budget. The necessary controls are straightforward and largely procedural. By spending a few hundred dollars on basic sensors and equipment, and dedicating management time to establishing simple policies and staff training, the café can drastically reduce its liability and secure its revenue stream.
